# Bench commissioning

Do not assume a successful compile establishes the pulse polarity, voltage, stop point, or behavior of a particular ESC. These steps verify the complete signal chain.

1. **Prepare:** remove propellers; lift wheels or otherwise unload and restrain the drivetrain. Disconnect motor power initially. Keep a physical motor-power cutoff available. Identify exact board, receiver, ESC, BEC voltage and signal connector pinouts from their manuals.
2. **Measure receiver:** use an oscilloscope between channel signal and ground. Record logic-high voltage, minimum/neutral/maximum pulse widths and repetition period. Confirm individual-channel PWM, not a serial bus. Choose input conversion from [wiring.md](wiring.md). Never use a multimeter's average pulse voltage as the logic-high value.
3. **Wire and flash:** power the MCU by USB, add the input converter and common ground, leave the ESC signal disconnected. Flash the correct board target. Use the documented output pulldown. Connect the app and Read board. With receiver inputs absent it should show NO SIGNAL with stopped outputs.
4. **Calibrate input:** read live pulse values at receiver minimum, neutral and maximum. Enter those endpoints, deadband, board GPIOs, and the ESC's documented pulse calibration. Default 1500 µs stop is only for ESCs that actually use it. Set bidirectional mode, initially choose 10–30% per direction, Apply RAM and Save flash. Reboot, reconnect and confirm the saved settings match.
5. **Check output with a scope:** at boot, keep an input away from neutral and confirm all outputs remain at configured stop. Verify voltage and the 20 ms period. Hold every input at neutral for one second; control must enable automatically. Move slowly in each direction. Default 1000/1500/2000 calibration with 30% scale gives endpoints 1350 and 1650 µs. Test asymmetric limits, clamp mode and inversion.
6. **Verify neutral gating:** reboot at forward/reverse throttle and confirm outputs remain stopped. A neutral interval shorter than one second must not enable control. Once active, Apply/Save/Rename at throttle must be rejected; holding all inputs neutral for one second must permit them. Every accepted mutation must stop outputs and require a new neutral dwell.
7. **Verify receiver failsafe:** while active and unloaded, disconnect each receiver signal or remove receiver power. All outputs must stop after timeout and a subsequent PWM frame. Restore signal at throttle: outputs must remain stopped. Return every input to neutral for one second: control must resume automatically. Test stuck-high/low lines, bad pulse widths and both directions.
8. **Verify radio-loss behavior separately:** turn off the transmitter while the receiver stays powered. A receiver that holds its last valid throttle pulse is invisible to this limiter's pulse-loss test. Program its failsafe to transmit neutral/stop or remove pulses, then confirm what happens. A valid neutral failsafe produces neutral but can leave automatic control active; loss/invalid pulses stop outputs and reset the neutral dwell. Do not use receiver "hold last" for throttle.
9. **Verify ESC missing-signal behavior:** with motor unloaded, remove the MCU signal/power and observe that the ESC stops. Test MCU restart, USB reconnect, BEC brownout, and the potential two-second watchdog delay separately. Hardware PWM may retain its last pulse if the main loop stalls; the watchdog is a recovery measure, not an immediate stop circuit.
10. **Connect the ESC:** remove motor power, connect the verified output (direct or buffered as required), then power the ESC normally with the transmitter at neutral. Let the ESC perform its own arming sequence according to its manual. Allow the one-second neutral dwell to complete and test modest command in both directions. Automotive brake-then-reverse logic, drag brake and input deadbands are ESC-specific. Reducing pulse excursion does not override those settings.
11. **Verify each transport:** exercise USB and Bluetooth, cable removal, app closure and Android backgrounding. App disconnect does not stop receiver control; no app heartbeat is required. Confirm transmitter stop and the physical cutoff remain usable.

Calibrate full ESC endpoints according to its manual before relying on a limit. Do not run ESC endpoint learning through an already limited output: the ESC may learn the limited excursion as full power and defeat the intended limit. Recheck limits whenever ESC calibration, transmitter endpoints, trims, direction or receiver configuration changes.

## Additional checks for multiple mappings and serial transports

- Repeat calibration, voltage and waveform checks for every mapping. Check all outputs simultaneously, including when two Pico outputs use different channels of the same PWM slice.
- Give two outputs different limits and verify their measured excursions differ as configured. For shared-input mappings, verify both respond to the same receiver signal while retaining their individual limits.
- Hold all but one input neutral and the remaining input at throttle: all outputs must stay stopped after boot/fault. Then hold every input neutral for one second and confirm simultaneous activation.
- While active, disconnect each unique receiver input in turn. Every output must stop; restoration at throttle must not recover. Require all inputs neutral for another second. Draft changes must not affect live hardware before Apply all.
- Save several mappings, power-cycle and verify count, pins, all limits and initial stopped state. If upgrading from old firmware, first verify the previous one-channel saved configuration loads correctly; then Save to write version 2. Reinspect defaults if stored data could not be recovered.
- On Pico, confirm USB CDC can read/apply/save all mappings. On the original ESP32, repeat the same operations over USB-to-UART serial and over Bluetooth Classic SPP after OS pairing with the board’s RC-<device name>. Check the GUI's Bluetooth capability status. These transports should produce equivalent results, using one controller at a time.

This project does not detect actual speed, current, temperature, or a power-stage failure. If you need a maximum vehicle speed, use speed feedback and an appropriate controller; do not interpret a pulse percentage as a guaranteed speed. Record your measured pulse/voltage results and retest after wiring or firmware changes.

## C3, board layouts and resistor-divider checks

- Confirm the actual PCB matches the chosen [board layout](board-layouts.md), particularly a Super Mini clone's orientation, LED/BOOT pins and USB wiring. Check each suggested GPIO against the printed labels. Keep C3 straps 2/8/9, native USB 18/19 and UART 20/21 free.
- For a confirmed 5 V receiver high, measure the divider junction using 4.7 kΩ series and 6.8 kΩ to common ground: expect about 2.96 V nominal. Test min/max source voltage and power sequencing. Do not use the divider on a 3.3 V signal without checking thresholds; it falls to about 1.95 V. Measure actual edges with the cable and attached input.
- On C3, check all four PWM outputs with BLE traffic active and after USB reconnect. C3 LEDC uses 14-bit duty resolution at 50 Hz (about 1.22 µs quantization). Verify pulse jitter, neutral and stop-all behavior on the physical board.
- Build Super Mini with native CDC enabled and DevKitM-1 with the UART connector target. Verify the intended USB connector can read/apply/save settings and recover from unplug/replug.
- Connect BLE using the dedicated C3 BLE chooser; verify discovery, RX writes and TX notification subscription, four-channel fragmented replies, apply/save, long commands, reconnects, link loss, congestion and overflow/timeout recovery. Reconnect after a quarantined/overflowed BLE session. Repeat on a real Android phone, including permission denial and backgrounding.

- With outputs stopped or all inputs neutral for one second, save a device name and verify it survives USB reset and a settings-preserving firmware update. Check the unchanged hardware ID, next-boot Bluetooth name, continued BLE NUS discovery, and preservation of identity when saving/importing different mapping profiles. USB product/COM labels may remain generic.
